# Legal Considerations & the Free/Scraping Route for NBA Stats (Commercial Season-Long Fantasy App)

*Informational only — not legal advice. US law focus. Researched Nov 10, 2026. Note on method: direct page fetches of nba.com, sports-reference.com and tostracker.app were blocked by this session's network policy, so ToS findings rely on search-result excerpts rather than full verbatim reads. Primary-source verification of the NBA.com Terms of Use is still needed.*

## 1. Case law: player names + stats in fantasy (NBA v. Motorola, C.B.C., CBS v. NFLPA, Daniels, 2020s)

### Takeaway
US case law strongly supports the position that raw game statistics and player names, used factually in a fantasy game, are not owned by leagues/players: facts aren't copyrightable, "hot news" claims failed (Motorola), and right-of-publicity claims lose to the First Amendment (C.B.C., 8th Cir.) or statutory newsworthiness exceptions (Daniels, Indiana). The protection is strongest for names + stats; it does not extend to photos, logos, or anything implying endorsement — and 2024 MLBPA suits show unions still litigate image use.

### Cited Findings
- **NBA v. Motorola, 105 F.3d 841 (2d Cir. 1997):** Motorola and STATS did not unlawfully misappropriate NBA property by transmitting real-time NBA scores and statistics taken from TV/radio broadcasts; because they bore their own costs of gathering the information, they were not free-riding. The court recognized only a narrow "hot news" claim surviving Copyright Act preemption and set out a five-part test — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/105/841/598844/); [OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/105/f3d/841)
- **C.B.C. Distribution & Marketing v. MLB Advanced Media, 505 F.3d 818 (8th Cir. Oct. 16, 2007):** Even assuming a Missouri right-of-publicity claim was made out, the First Amendment protected C.B.C.'s use of MLB player names and statistics in fantasy games; the court stressed the information was readily available in the public domain and that the use implied no endorsement. Affirmed district court, 443 F. Supp. 2d 1077 (E.D. Mo. 2006) — [Studicata](https://studicata.com/case-briefs/case/c-b-c-distribution-v-major-league-baseball); [8th Cir. opinion PDF](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/07/10/063357P.pdf); [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2007/10/cbc-distribution--marketing-inc-v-major-league-b__)
- Supreme Court denied certiorari in C.B.C. on June 2, 2008, leaving the ruling intact — [Studicata summary](https://www.studicata.com/summaries/united-states-court-of-appeals-eighth-circuit/c-b-c-v-major-league-2007-7cbbfl); [SCOTUSblog brief](https://www.scotusblog.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2008/05/07-1099_bio.pdf)
- C.B.C. also mattered contractually: C.B.C. had been a prior licensee; the 8th Circuit addressed the license's no-challenge provisions in C.B.C.'s favor (summaries differ on details) — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/trademark/55030/first-amendment-rights-trump-publicity-rights)
- **CBS Interactive v. NFLPA, 259 F.R.D. 398 (D. Minn. Apr. 28, 2009):** Judge Ann Montgomery applied C.B.C. and held CBS could use NFL player names and stats in fantasy games without a license from the NFL or NFLPA. CBS chose Minnesota (8th Cir.) because C.B.C. had not been extended to other circuits. NFLPA filed notice of appeal May 28, 2009 — [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2009/05/cbs-interactive-inc-v-national-football-league-p__); [Sports Litigation Alert](https://sportslitigationalert.com/fantasy-sports-leagues-get-another-victory-against-players-union/); [CommLawBlog](https://www.commlawblog.com/2009/06/articles/intellectual-property/a-case-of-piling-on/)
- **Daniels v. FanDuel (Ind. Oct. 24, 2018):** On a certified question from the 7th Circuit, the Indiana Supreme Court held that the newsworthiness exception in Indiana's right-of-publicity statute covers online fantasy sports operators' use of (college) players' names, pictures and statistics, rejecting the argument that the exception doesn't reach commercial uses. The 7th Circuit then ended the case — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/indiana/supreme-court/2018/18s-cq-134.html); [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2018/11/daniels-v-fanduel); [Right of Publicity Roadmap](https://rightofpublicityroadmap.com/?p=367)
- Note Daniels involved daily fantasy (DraftKings/FanDuel) and included *pictures*, but the ruling is Indiana statutory law only — [Sports Litigation Alert](https://sportslitigationalert.com/the-indiana-supreme-courts-decision-in-daniels-v-fanduel-inc-what-it-means-for-right-of-publicity-law-and-the-future-of-online-sports-betting/)
- **2020s development — MLBPA suits (Sept. 2024):** MLB Players Inc./MLBPA sued FanDuel, DraftKings, bet365 and Underdog Fantasy, alleging use of images of nearly every active player to promote prop bets without a license. FanDuel settled in Nov. 2024 (and later partnered with MLB Players Inc. and OneTeam); the Underdog case appeared to be proceeding in New York as of late 2024 — [Covers (filing)](https://www.covers.com/industry/mlbpa-files-lawsuit-against-fanduel-draftkings-sept-17-2024); [Covers (settlement)](https://www.covers.com/industry/mlbpa-fanduel-settle-lawsuit-over-name-image-likeness-use-nov-16-2024); [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/?p=202327); [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/mlb-players-union-sues-gambling-sites-including-draftkings-bet365-over-use-of-players-images-for-bets)

### Inferences
- A season-long fantasy app using NBA player names and box-score stats is on well-trodden, favorable legal ground (the C.B.C./CBS line is directly on point for season-long fantasy). The 2024 MLBPA suits targeted *images* used in promotion/betting, consistent with the line between facts (free) and likeness/photos (licensed).
- C.B.C. binds only the 8th Circuit and Daniels only Indiana; other states' publicity statutes vary, so the risk isn't zero but no court found here has ruled against fantasy use of names+stats.
- Practical lesson from C.B.C.: if you ever sign a license, read no-challenge/post-term clauses carefully.

### Gaps
- Outcome of the NFLPA appeal in CBS Interactive (likely settled/dismissed; not confirmed).
- 2025–2026 status of MLBPA v. Underdog/DraftKings/bet365 not found.
- No NBA-specific or NBPA-specific fantasy litigation in the 2020s found.

## 2. Licenses for headshots, team logos, NBA marks, group player likeness (NBPA / OneTeam / PLYRS UNTD) & costs

### Takeaway
Stats/names are free to use, but official headshots (copyrighted photos), team logos and NBA marks (trademarks owned via NBA Properties), and group player likeness (NBPA) all require licenses. The NBPA does **not** use OneTeam; it runs group licensing in-house, now under PLYRS UNTD (launched June 2026, replacing THINK450). No public price list exists; the only fee benchmark found is an old NFLPA fantasy deal at 7.5% of fantasy revenue.

### Cited Findings
- NBA Properties, Inc. controls merchandising and licensing for all 30 teams; no public/blanket license exists for logos or likeness — permission is negotiated case-by-case — [fea-game "hoop" legal notes](https://fea-game.github.io/hoop/archived/fantasy-simulation/2-legal) (informal developer source)
- A developer reported contacting the NBA for months with no response about permission to use NBA content in an app — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/130430)
- Retired players are licensed through the National Basketball Retired Players Association, separately — [fea-game legal notes](https://fea-game.github.io/hoop/archived/fantasy-simulation/2-legal)
- NBPA declined (2019) to join OneTeam Partners' effort to aggregate player rights; OneTeam is the licensing arm for MLBPA/NFLPA and others — [Sports Business Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessdaily.com/Journal/Issues/2019/11/25/Marketing-and-Sponsorship/OneTeam.aspx); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/06/16/redbird-capital-selling-stake-in-2-billion-nfl-mlb-players-union-business.html)
- NBPA regained group licensing rights in collective bargaining (2017 CBA); group licensing covers use of names/numbers/images of three or more players; THINK450 was formed 2017 to run it — [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/nba-launches-think450-to-handle-marketing-and-licensing-of-nba-players); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/sport/677046/national-basketball-players-association-launches-think450-to-handle-marketing-and-licensing-of-nba-players)
- **June 2026:** NBPA launched PLYRS UNTD, replacing THINK450, to manage collective NIL rights of 500+ NBA players (licensing, merchandising, digital content, etc.); CEO David Kelly. NBPA licensing generated $291M in 2024 per IRS filings — [Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/news/nbpa-revamps-commercial-arm-with-plyrs-untd-launch/); [License Global](https://www.licenseglobal.com/sports/inside-the-nbpa-s-launch-of-plyrs-untd); [NBPA](https://nbpa.com/news/plyrs-untd-nbpa-launches-new-commercial-brand-to-amplify-collective-player-influence); [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/basketball/2026/nba-players-union-plyrs-untd-1234936539/)
- THINK450 previously licensed Sorare (NFT fantasy game) — [Licensing International](https://licensinginternational.org/?p=61744)
- Cost benchmark (non-NBA, dated): a 2003 NFLPA fantasy deal required the operator to pay 7.5% of all revenue attributable to fantasy activity — [Willamette law paper](https://digitalcollections.willamette.edu/bitstreams/7a56d8df-94d7-4acc-8005-035f5e612d0e/download)
- NBA's official data rights: Sportradar is the exclusive worldwide provider of NBA/WNBA/G League data from 2023-24 through 2030-31 (deal announced Nov. 2021, relationship since 2016); FanDuel gets official NBA data via Sportradar through 2030-31 — [NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-sportradar-announce-landmark-long-term-global-partnership); [SportBusiness](https://www.sportbusiness.com/news/sportradar-fanduel-extend-nba-data-agreement/)

### Inferences
- MVP path: names + stats + generic/own visuals (initials avatars, team abbreviations/colors without logos). Pulling headshots from cdn.nba.com is using copyrighted NBA photos without a license; displaying team logos is trademark use — both are the riskiest elements, and the ones rightsholders actually sue over.
- Licensed headshots are often bundled by commercial data providers (Sportradar images, etc.) — another reason to budget for a provider rather than piecing together scraped assets.
- Any official license (PLYRS UNTD for group likeness; NBA Properties for marks) should be expected to involve royalties/minimum guarantees; with no public NBA figures, the 7.5% NFLPA benchmark is only directional.

### Gaps
- No published fee schedule or minimum guarantee for NBA Properties, NBPA/PLYRS UNTD fantasy licenses, or NBA photo licensing.
- Whether PLYRS UNTD has a standard fantasy-sports licensing program is not public.

## 3. NBA.com / stats.nba.com terms, enforcement, and nba_api reliability

### Takeaway
NBA.com's Terms of Use govern all NBA digital platforms (operated in part by Turner Digital Basketball Services); exact scraping/commercial-use clauses could not be retrieved verbatim this session, but stats.nba.com is an undocumented, unsupported API. The larger practical problem is technical: requests from cloud/datacenter IPs (AWS, Azure, Heroku, GitHub Actions, Colab) commonly hang or time out, likely due to Akamai bot protection — making nba_api unreliable as a production backend.

### Cited Findings
- NBA.com terms apply to "access and use of the digital platforms of the National Basketball Association" and use constitutes acceptance; one tracker lists the operator as Turner Digital Basketball Services, Inc. Relevant automated-access clauses were not visible in retrieved excerpts — [NBA.com Terms](https://www.nba.com/terms); [ToS Tracker](https://tostracker.app/document/nba/version/388714)
- NBA content terms of service also exist at content.nba.com — [NBA content ToS](https://content.nba.com/terms_of_service)
- nba_api GitHub issue #176 (read timeouts, closed Feb 2021): commenters suggest NBA blocks calls from cloud providers such as AWS and Google Colab; issue #407 (Nov 2023) reported further timeouts — [nba_api issue #176 mirror](https://github.lx164.workers.dev/swar/nba_api/issues/176); [nba_api issues](https://WWW.GITHUB.COM/swar/nba_api/issues)
- Other NBA-API client projects report NBA "has blacklisted certain blocks of IP addresses," specifically cloud hosting providers including AWS, and suggest running a pass-through proxy somewhere not on AWS/Heroku — [bttmly/nba](https://www.github.com/bttmly/nba); [nba.js issue #16](https://www.github.com/kshvmdn/nba.js/issues/16)
- Timeouts in GitHub Actions workflows and Azure web apps reported; extra headers fixed it locally but not when deployed — [Stack Overflow mirror](https://kiwix.lema.org/stackoverflow.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/73066785/nba-api-timeouts-when-run-with-github-workflow); [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/1664241/http-timeout-in-azure-python-web-app)
- HN comment / third-party client: stats.nba.com sits behind Akamai bot protection that rejects requests based on TLS fingerprints, silently dropping datacenter IP connections; live endpoints reportedly on a different, less-protected host (anecdotal) — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/threads?id=gek0z); [pkgstats nba-api-ts](https://www.pkgstats.com/pkg:nba-api-ts)
- nba_api added per-request proxy, headers, and timeout support (v1.1.9) — [PyPI nba_api 1.1.9](https://pypi.org/project/nba-api/1.1.9/)
- Secondary guide: stats.nba.com aggressively rate-limits rapid requests, typically returning HTTP 429 or empty responses — [Brown VR Wiki](https://www.vrwiki.cs.brown.edu/applications-of-vr/vr-in-sports/troubleshooting-tips-for-nba-apiprivacy-laws-for-nba-api)

### Inferences
- Running nba_api from a cloud backend will likely require residential proxies or a non-cloud relay — which is exactly the kind of circumvention that strengthens breach-of-contract / ToS claims and looks bad if challenged.
- No official SLA, documentation, or change notice: endpoints and parameters change without warning, so a commercial product's live scoring could break mid-season.
- The NBA has an exclusive data deal with Sportradar through 2030-31, giving it commercial incentive to resist free-riding by commercial apps, even if formal suits against scrapers aren't public.

### Gaps
- Verbatim NBA.com ToS language on robots/scraping/commercial use (fetch blocked; must be read directly at nba.com/termsofuse).
- No public record found of NBA suing or sending C&Ds to scrapers/nba_api users; absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
- No authoritative rate-limit numbers for stats.nba.com.
- Broader scraping case law (hiQ v. LinkedIn, Van Buren CFAA, Meta/X v. Bright Data) not researched in this pass — relevant to whether public-page scraping is a CFAA issue vs. merely a contract issue.

## 4. Other free data sources (Basketball-Reference / Sports Reference, ESPN hidden APIs) and their ToS

### Takeaway
Neither is a legitimate free feed for a commercial app. Sports Reference explicitly says not to build websites or tools from data scraped from its sites, offers no API (citing its own data licensing agreements), rate-limits and blocks bots. ESPN's "hidden API" is undocumented and unsupported, can change without notice, and commentators advise against using it for a commercial product.

### Cited Findings
- Sports Reference data use page: "you should not create websites or tools based on data you scrape from Sports Reference or any of our sites or use our data to train generative artificial intelligence models without our permission" — [SR Data Use](https://www.sports-reference.com/data_use.html)
- SR says it cannot provide an API per its data licensing agreements (i.e., its own data is licensed in from upstream providers) — [SR Data Use](https://www.sports-reference.com/data_use.html)
- SR terms bar using automated means to access the site in a manner that adversely impacts performance or access without express written permission; SR implements rate limiting and proactively blocks badly behaved bots — [SR Bot Traffic](https://www.sports-reference.com/bot-traffic.html); [SR blog](https://www.sports-reference.com/blog/category/announcement/page/8/)
- Community: sportsipy users hit SR request limits and needed delays between calls — [sportsipy issue #756](https://www.github.com/roclark/sportsipy/issues/756)
- (Commonly cited figure of ~20 requests/minute for SR could not be confirmed in this pass.)
- ESPN hidden API: no official documentation or developer support; endpoints can be modified or removed at any time without notice; heavy automated use may violate ESPN's ToS; for commercial products, license from ESPN or a data provider — [Zuplo](https://zuplo.com/learning-center/espn-hidden-api-guide); [sportsapis.dev](https://sportsapis.dev/espn-api)
- ESPN shut down its public API key program in 2014 after previously limiting APIs to commercial partners — [App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/the-world-wide-leader-in-sports-leaves-developers-in-a-world-of-hurt-as-espn-closes-public-api/amp/); [TechCrunch 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/03/05/espn-developer-center-and-apis)

### Inferences
- The *facts* (box scores) are free to use, but *how you obtain them* is governed by each site's contract terms. Scraping SR or ESPN for a commercial app is a clear ToS breach with a real chance of IP blocks; legal exposure is mostly contract-based.
- Legally cleanest "free" route is self-collecting facts from public sources (e.g., your own entry from broadcasts — the Motorola model) or a low-cost licensed API; in practice a paid provider is cheaper than engineering around blocks.

### Gaps
- ESPN's current ToS text on automated access not retrieved.
- Other free/freemium sources (balldontlie, API-Sports free tier) are outside this note's scope — covered by pricing researchers.

## 5. Hot news misappropriation and near-real-time stats

### Takeaway
Hot-news is a weak threat to a fantasy app that gathers or buys its own data. Motorola itself ruled *against* the NBA on real-time scores, and Barclays v. Theflyonthewall (2d Cir. 2011) narrowed the doctrine further, treating Motorola's test as dicta and finding the claim preempted. Residual risk exists mainly for direct free-riding on the NBA's own real-time feed in a way that threatens its product.

### Cited Findings
- Motorola: a narrow hot-news claim survives preemption only where (paraphrasing the five-part test) the plaintiff gathers time-sensitive information at cost, the defendant free-rides on it, the defendant directly competes, and free-riding would threaten the plaintiff's incentive to produce; here the defendants paid their own collection costs, so no liability — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/105/841/598844/); [Casemine commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/nba-v.-motorola:-defining-the-limits-of-hot-news-misappropriation-in-real-time-data-transmission/view)
- Barclays Capital v. Theflyonthewall.com (2d Cir. 2011): reversed a hot-news injunction; held the claim preempted by copyright law; called Motorola's five-part test non-binding dicta; commentators said it "erodes" the doctrine — [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-1978); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/sites/default/files/files/download/publications/alert_court_rejects_hot_news_protection.pdf); [Federalist Society Review](https://fedsoc.org/fedsoc-review/hot-news-the-hot-news-doctrine-is-hot-again-or-is-it)

### Inferences
- A season-long fantasy app updating scores post-game or with modest delay is far from the hot-news paradigm; even live scoring was held permissible in Motorola where the defendant did its own collection.
- Scraping the NBA's own live feed (stats/cdn endpoints) is the closest case to "free-riding on the plaintiff's costly gathering"; the practical exposure there is more ToS/contract than hot-news, but it's the fact pattern to avoid.
- Hot-news is a state-law doctrine (mainly NY); its strength outside the 2d Circuit wasn't researched.

### Gaps
- Treatment of hot-news in circuits other than the 2nd.
- Whether any post-2011 sports-data case has successfully used hot-news (none found).
